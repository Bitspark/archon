# Code of conduct

archon is a small technical project, and this page is short for the same reason
CONTRIBUTING.md is: the rules are few, and each is here because its absence costs
something.

## What is expected

Discuss the work, not the person. Argue from the code, the RFCs and the vectors — a claim
about what a change does should be one somebody else can check, and in this repository
most of them can be, by running the harness. Say plainly when you are uncertain, and
plainly when you are wrong; neither costs anything here.

Accept that a change may be declined. The scope rule declines a good many of them, and
declining one is not a judgement about who proposed it.

## What is not

Harassment of any kind, sexual attention, insults, and demeaning or derogatory remarks
about a person or a group — including about anyone's experience, nationality, gender,
sexuality, religion, disability or appearance. Publishing anyone's private information.
Persisting after being asked to stop. Deliberately derailing the work of others.

This holds in issues, pull requests, commit messages, code and every other space the
project occupies, and it holds when representing the project elsewhere.

## Reporting and enforcement

Report a concern privately to the maintainers, through **[a private report on the
repository's Security tab][private-report]**. That form is meant for vulnerabilities and is
used here for conduct as well, deliberately: it is the private channel this repository has,
it reaches the maintainers and nobody else, and naming no address was preferred to
inventing one. Say what happened and where; you do not have to have a remedy in mind. A
report is not shared beyond the people needed to act on it, and the person who reports is
not identified to the person reported without their agreement.

The maintainers answer within five working days. They may edit or remove a contribution,
ask for a change in behavior, or bar someone from the project's spaces, in proportion to
what happened and to whether it is a pattern. A maintainer who is the subject of a report
takes no part in handling it.

Conduct that GitHub itself governs — spam, impersonation, threats — can also be reported to
GitHub.

[private-report]: https://github.com/Bitspark/archon/security/advisories/new
